BLACK BOX — PRODUCT AND LAUNCH PLAN
==================================
Prepared for Dennis Rogers | November 22, 2026
Companion to the v0.9.0+12 source update

DECISION
--------
Build around a clear family job: agree on a plan, give the right people a
complete copy, and keep that copy understandable when service is unavailable.
The rugged field-terminal design supports that job. Dependable access,
straightforward setup, and clear sharing should determine the product scope.

This document separates implemented source changes, proposed product work,
and requirements verified from primary sources. Competitor features are their
published claims, reviewed on November 22, 2026; their apps were not installed,
security-audited, or independently tested. These findings do not establish
competitor revenue, demand, or Black Box's future profitability.

The project targets below are proposed decision points. They are not promises
of a launch date, an earnings forecast, or industry conversion benchmarks.


1. WHAT WAS BUILT IN THIS UPDATE
--------------------------------
LOCAL PLAN LIBRARY
- A selector below the main header identifies the selected local plan.
- Create an independent blank plan with You as its first member.
- Give household, extended-family, travel, and other plans separate people,
  meeting places, instructions, family information, and shared packing lists.
- Duplicate a plan without changing its source. Shared-kit Packed checks are
  cleared in the duplicate so the new kit can be checked separately.
- Import an existing backup as a new plan. Import preserves its packed values;
  review them because the file may describe an older state of the supplies.
- Archive a plan reversibly and restore it to the active list. The current
  plan must be switched before it can be archived. This is not deletion.
- Keep independent current, previous, and interrupted-restore records.

FAMILY INFORMATION
- An out-of-area contact's name and phone number.
- Additional contacts with name, role, and phone number.
- The household's communication instructions.
- Care and pickup responsibilities for children, relatives, pets, or people
  needing assistance, written by the user.
- Departure instructions agreed on by the household.
- A readable summary on Home and full information in Plan.
- The selected plan's JSON backup and readable text copy include these fields.

DATA AND INSTRUCTIONS
- The original plan keeps its existing storage keys. Registering it in the
  library does not rewrite its saved plan or recovery bytes.
- A failed initial library-directory write can leave the original plan open
  for reading, with a warning and retry path. An unreadable existing directory
  is never silently reset.
- Unsaved changes must save successfully before another plan can be selected.
- New plan records are written before the directory selects them. Failure can
  leave an unlisted new record, but does not delete existing plans.
- Plan schema 3 reads older schema 1/2 plans, adding empty family information.
  A previous raw copy is retained before the first schema-3 edit.
- Old app versions cannot read the new format. Keep a pre-update backup and
  use v0.9 or a compatible newer release for the updated data.
- The six-part tour now explains multiple plans and family information;
  searchable Help has twelve topics.
- My pack and the personal guide library remain common across local plans.
  They are not included in an individual group-plan JSON backup.

This is source prepared for the next build. Pure-Dart data checks and source
review do not establish Flutter compilation, successful iPhone installation,
accessibility, or actual offline playback. Follow the included build and
manual-check instructions. Cloud accounts, invitations, subscriptions, forum,
inbox, licensed offline maps, and routing are not implemented in this package.


2. WHAT THE COMPETITOR REVIEW CHANGES
-------------------------------------
PrepperApp: publishes household invitations, shared supplies/plans, offline
guides and routes, named places, printable maps, and a free-posting forum.
The overall combination is already present in the market. [C1, C2]

Firefly: describes guided family planning, offline information, family
contacts and documents, with continued access to saved information after a
trial and a one-time payment for further editing. Free reading/paid editing
is already offered elsewhere; recurring cloud collaboration must earn a
subscription's ongoing value. [C3]

ReadyPlan: describes scenario plans, alternate routes and communication
plans, checklists, selective sharing, and saved offline plans. Its PDF wording
is inconsistent between its description and release notes, so PDF availability
was not independently resolved. [C4]

Our Plan Is: describes people/pets, outside contacts, go-bags, offline household
plans, later synchronization, and attributed public-safety information. These
are useful comparisons for a product aimed at ordinary households. [C5]

Warrior Gardeners: describes separate locations/inventories, expiration
tracking, scenarios, practice records, and per-resource sharing choices.
Plan maintenance and understandable audiences deserve explicit design. [C6]

SHTF: Life Prep: describes planning, private groups, chat, a community guide
library, expiration tracking, and offline saved plans/guides/inventory.
Community adds an operational service to maintain, not just another screen. [C7]

Blackout: Emergency Plan: describes offline household tools, a family card,
expiration reminders, encrypted backups, and printable plans. Its nearby
messaging is described as operating over tens of meters; that does not solve
communication between relatives in different states. [C8]

POSITIONING HYPOTHESIS TO TEST
Black Box can be a clear, durable family-planning tool with rugged presentation
and understandable access across several groups. A future public promise of
family-wide offline access should follow successful cross-device testing.
Do not claim that the feature combination is unique, that a downloaded plan
proves safety, or that the aircraft-inspired name means certified protection.


3. DEFINE A FIRST CUSTOMER AND A COMPLETE WORKFLOW
-------------------------------------------------
Start with one organizing adult in a household who wants relatives to know
where to go, what to bring, who needs assistance, and what to do if they cannot
communicate. Include a less technical relative in each pilot group. Designing
only for the founder's own comfort with technology would miss the hard part.

The first shared-plan workflow should be:
1. The organizer creates a clear plan and adds permitted guide files.
2. They invite a specific person and choose that person's access.
3. The invitee accepts, reviews the audience, and downloads the complete plan.
4. The app confirms the locally saved revision and the files actually present.
5. The invitee turns off service, closes/reopens the app, and reads the plan
   and supported guide files without paying for an editor subscription.
6. An update is downloaded without destroying the previous complete copy.

For relatives in another state, a meeting plan also needs user-written
contingencies: when travel should wait, which alternate applies, and what to
do when the primary place cannot be reached. The app should store the family's
agreed instructions, not infer current road safety or invent evacuation orders.


4. BUILD ORDER AND ACCEPTANCE CONDITIONS
----------------------------------------
STAGE A — LOCAL FAMILY PRODUCT (CURRENT SOURCE INCREMENT)
Multiple local plans and family information are implemented in v0.9.
Next: compile, check the screens, and exercise migration/switching with real
copies of the user's existing data. Check Light/Dark, larger text, long names,
empty plans, imported plans, and unsuccessful saves on actual devices.

STAGE B — DURABLE OFFLINE COPIES AND PORTABILITY
Before broad release, migrate primary plan data from prototype preferences
to a transactional local store, retain recovery copies, and test interrupted
migration. Design local protection and key recovery deliberately. The current
prototype must not be advertised as an encrypted vault.

Add native phone backup export/share, selected-plan and whole-library restore
previews, and a printable concise family card plus full-plan copy. Explain
whether a file includes contacts, guides, personal inventory, and map packages.
Do not let an export button imply that every asset is present in its output.

Add visible last-reviewed and last-practiced dates, notes, and optional review
reminders. Record actual user actions. Add expiry/replacement dates and kit
locations to inventory. Give people useful maintenance tasks without implying
that a calculated percentage establishes their ability to survive an event.

Red Cross guidance supports written contacts, an out-of-area contact, assigned
responsibilities, meeting places, and practicing the plan. Use authoritative
guidance to inform product fields and licensed content; clinical/survival
instructions need appropriate sourcing and review. [P1]

STAGE C — PRIVATE SHARING AND A FREE READER DOWNLOAD
Implement accounts, accepted invitations, plan membership and permissions,
server-enforced access, and versioned cloud copies. Test one organizer and one
free reader on separate devices before expanding the feature surface.

Each plan needs its own owner and access list. Membership in Group A does not
grant access to Group B. Paying for Premium does not grant access to someone
else's plan. Display the audience before any plan/file is shared.

Use separate states for editing, saved locally, uploading, uploaded, downloading,
and downloaded. Show the saved revision and time; an offline device cannot
know that it has the newest server revision. Download into a temporary package,
verify required files, and promote the complete package. An interrupted update
must leave the previous complete copy readable.

Resolve simultaneous edits deliberately. Preserve conflicting changes and
explain who changed what. A last-write-wins policy can silently lose a changed
meeting place, which is unacceptable for the product's central purpose.

Keep an already downloaded emergency copy readable during connectivity loss,
temporary authentication failure, and subscription expiry. Revoking access
stops future server access; explain that already downloaded/exported copies
cannot be guaranteed remotely erased.

STAGE D — SUBSCRIPTIONS WITH MEASURABLE ONGOING VALUE
Keep the agreed rules: free authorized readers/downloaders, free personal
packing, and Premium for authorized shared-plan editing/contributing. Editing
requires both the subscription entitlement and plan permission.

Start with development entitlements and sandbox purchases, then verified store
transactions, renewal/expiry/refund handling, purchase restoration, and a clear
subscription-management path. The first iPhone implementation should use
Apple's subscription tools; prices, periods and included service must be clear.
Apple describes subscriptions as ongoing access with ongoing value. [A3, A4]

STAGE E — INBOX, THEN A MANAGED COMMUNITY
Build the requested subject-based Inbox/Sent/Drafts/Archive model. Offline
composition is a draft or queue; it is not a delivered message. Downloaded
messages may remain readable, but new remote delivery still needs a supported
communication path. Long-distance communication without infrastructure is
not a capability of this app.

For the forum: free posting/replies remain the plan. Moderators need the
assigned role and the agreed Premium eligibility; purchasing alone never
grants moderation. Keep reporting, blocking and privacy controls available.
Apple's user-content rules require filtering, reports with timely handling,
blocking and published contact information. Assign a person to operations
before exposing the service to the public. [A1]

STAGE F — LICENSED OFFLINE MAPS AND ROUTING
Choose a provider/package whose rights permit offline downloading. The standard
OpenStreetMap tile endpoint prohibits bulk/offline prefetch. A different
permitted service or self-hosted approach is required. [M1]

Map imagery, route calculation, and live hazards are different capabilities.
Test region boundaries, storage limits, updates, missing routing data, and
travel mode. Let people place an origin manually as requested. Keep written
destinations/instructions accessible even if the map or route is unavailable.
Do not label a route safe merely because the routing engine can calculate it.


5. REQUIRED LAUNCH OPERATIONS
------------------------------
DEVICE TESTING
Use the spare iPhone for install, cold restart, airplane mode with Wi-Fi off,
PDF opening, supported video playback, background/foreground transitions,
low storage, interrupted import/download, and app-update checks. Add at least
one smaller or older supported device before promising broad go-bag support.
Verify the eventual minimum OS/device requirements against the actual build.

TestFlight is a beta distribution method. Apple says builds become unavailable
after 90 days. It is useful for pilot testing and unsuitable as the permanent
go-bag installation. Use the external tester process for pilot households;
they do not need developer-account access. [A5]

PRIVACY AND SUPPORT
Provide a real support address, an accurate policy, and a way to report a
problem. Inventory what maps, storage services, purchases and any SDKs send
off-device before completing App Store privacy answers. Apple requires the
answers to account for integrated third parties and remain current. [A6]

When accounts exist, add an in-app account-deletion path and explain data
retention. Handle shared information and ownership transfer deliberately.
Account deletion and Apple subscription cancellation are separate actions. [A7]

For shared guide files: choose accepted types/size limits, validate content,
restrict access, and establish deletion/retention and abuse handling. A user
having a PDF/video does not automatically give Black Box rights to distribute
it publicly. Personal imported files should not silently become community posts.

Add a support/incident procedure, server recovery backups, access logging that
does not expose private plan contents, and a way to roll back an unsuccessful
release. Someone must be responsible for reports and outages.

BRAND CHECK
Keep Black Box as the working name. There is an existing Blackbox puzzle app
and a preparedness app called Blackout. This is discoverability/confusion
evidence, not a conclusion of infringement or legal clearance. [B1, C8]
Before committing substantial branding spend, check names, stores, domains,
and relevant trademark/common-law uses. USPTO describes a comprehensive
clearance process that goes beyond its federal database. [B2]


6. MONEY: WHAT TO VALIDATE BEFORE CHOOSING A PRICE
-------------------------------------------------
Ongoing private collaboration, storage, version history and shared downloads
can support a recurring offer. A permanent charge to reopen an already saved
emergency plan would conflict with the agreed reader-access promise.

Measure the economics for the entire group supported by each payer:
Monthly contribution = subscription receipts after store deductions/refunds
minus storage, downloads, maps, messages, support, moderation and other costs.

Video storage and repeat downloads need explicit allowances. Do not promise
unlimited cloud videos or lifetime cloud service before knowing the cost.
Affiliate revenue is an optional additional stream, not a substitute for a
working paid-product model. Keep the existing approval/disclosure work separate.

Illustration only: 500 paying users at a hypothetical $6/month would produce
$3,000/month in gross billings before deductions and costs. This is arithmetic,
not a forecast, recommended price, profit estimate, or expected customer count.
Do not use subscriber counts alone to decide whether the business is healthy.

Interviews can identify expectations, but willingness to pay should ultimately
be checked against a real working offer. Track cancellations and reasons,
support work per group, download/storage cost, and continued use after setup.
Choose sustainable spending limits now; expand spending after evidence improves.


7. A FAMILY-LED ORGANIC LAUNCH
------------------------------
The founder story is a parent building a clearer way for the family to prepare.
Dennis and his family can show actual use and what they learn while building.
The most persuasive demonstration is another household succeeding with the
same workflow. The adults can carry the story; children's identities are not
needed to demonstrate the product.

Use a separate demonstration plan with fictional contact details and locations.
Avoid exposing home/school addresses, pickup arrangements, private documents,
or supply-storage locations in screenshots and videos. Say that the app is
yours when that ownership is not otherwise clear. FTC guidance addresses
material relationships and misleading endorsements. [G1]

PROPOSED FIRST CONTENT SERIES
1. Introduce the problem: does everyone in the household know the same plan?
2. Build a simple demonstration plan and show the written meeting instructions.
3. Show Owned versus Packed using ordinary household supplies.
4. Explain who helps whom, using a fictional family-information card.
5. Show importing a real permitted PDF/video and opening its saved copy.
6. After device testing succeeds, show a genuine offline close/reopen exercise.
7. Show something a pilot family found confusing and the improvement made.

Use a clear stage-appropriate call to action: volunteer for a pilot, join a
waitlist, or download the released app. Do not advertise unbuilt offline maps,
automated family alerts, encrypted storage, or cross-device sharing as finished.

Consider a simple landing page with accurate current capabilities, the founder
story, sample screens, privacy/support information, and a consent-based pilot
signup. No landing page, mailing list, tracking service, posts, or invitations
were deployed or sent as part of this update.

PROPOSED VALIDATION MILESTONES
- Interview 10 households about their existing plans and real gaps. Ask what
  they currently do before showing a long feature list.
- Recruit 10–15 pilot households, including people who are less technical and
  at least one group with relatives living elsewhere.
- Observe setup without coaching. Record completed steps, errors and where
  people need help. Improve the product before adding more acquisition volume.
- Require every pilot's supported device to pass the offline retrieval exercise;
  fix every critical data-loss/access failure before expanding.
- Prove an invited free reader receives the correct plan while an unrelated
  account cannot access it. Test the server rules, not just hidden UI buttons.
- Revisit pilots after about 30 days: did they review or update the plan, invite
  someone, and still understand what their device holds?
- Test a real paid offer once its benefits work, then measure conversion,
  continued subscriptions, support burden and per-group cost before buying ads.

These sample sizes and times are internal project targets. They do not prove
market demand statistically and are not established performance benchmarks.


8. NEXT CONCRETE MILESTONE
---------------------------
Upload v0.9.0+12 and run a fresh ios-compile-check. In the working preview,
create two differently named plans, add different family information and
supplies, switch/reopen them, import a backup as a third plan, and test archive
and restore. Verify that the personal pack and guide library remain available.

When Apple enrollment becomes active, finish signing/TestFlight setup and
perform the phone checks. Then complete native portability and the private
organizer-to-free-reader download flow. A public launch date should follow
those demonstrated outcomes, rather than determine whether they are skipped.

No cloud service, billing product, moderation operation, trademark clearance,
marketing account, or public deployment was created in this source update.
Those steps need the actual owned accounts, configuration and operational
choices. The current prototype remains a local development product.
