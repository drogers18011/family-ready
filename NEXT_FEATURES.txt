3. GROUPS, PLANS, PEOPLE, AND PERMISSIONS
-----------------------------------------
These are separate things:
- Account: a signed-in Black Box user.
- Group: an invitation-based collection of accounts.
- Plan: an independently owned set of instructions, places, people, supplies,
  assignments, and supported attachments.
- Person in a plan: someone named in the plan, who may not have an account.
  A child, relative, or other named participant is not automatically a user
  with permission to sign in or access the plan.
- Plan grant: permission for an account or selected group to read a plan,
  plus explicit Editor permission where the owner allows it.
- Subscription entitlement: verified permission to use paid capabilities.
- Community role: a scoped Moderator assignment or the separate operator
  administration role. Neither Premium nor a group invitation grants it.

Example:
- Rogers family group: Home evacuation plan and storm shelter plan.
- Hunting group: Hunting-camp plan.
- Extended family group: Interstate meeting plan shared with selected members.
- Dennis alone: a private personal plan, not automatically shared anywhere.

A plan can be shared with more than one group while retaining one plan ID
and one current published revision. Copying a plan is a separate action:
the copy gets a new ID and does not silently follow later changes.

RECOMMENDED INITIAL PERMISSION RULES
- Plans begin private.
- Inviting a person to a group does not share every private plan owned by
  other members.
- Share a named plan with selected people or a selected group explicitly.
  If sharing with an entire group, explain whether future members are included.
- Newly invited readers start as Viewers.
- The plan owner chooses Editors. Group administration and plan ownership
  are separate; administering a group does not expose someone's private plan.
- Permission to edit does not automatically include permission to change
  the sharing list or transfer ownership.
- Buying Premium never upgrades a Viewer grant into an Editor grant.

Effective shared-plan write rule:
  signed in + current plan edit permission + active paid entitlement
  + permission for that specific operation + valid base revision

The server must enforce the rule. Hiding an Edit button is only a UI choice.
Backups must never grant account membership, sharing permission, or paid status.

FREE VERSUS PREMIUM
- Free authorized Viewer: read and download the complete plan; receive newer
  published revisions while still authorized; no shared content edits.
- Premium Viewer: the same read access, but still cannot edit that plan until
  its owner grants Editor permission.
- Premium Editor: change content within the granted plan scope.
- Owner with expired Premium: keep reading and downloading, and keep access
  to privacy/security actions such as removing access or deleting the account.
  Content editing stops unless paid entitlement is active.
- Another authorized Premium Editor may keep contributing even if the owner's
  subscription expires. One person's renewal must not disable family reading.
- All users: create/edit their personal packing list and mark their own items
  packed without Premium. Those actions do not update group supplies.
- Free community participant: create topics/replies, report content, and block
  users; no moderation powers.
- Premium community participant: the same posting rights; no moderation powers
  unless an authorized administrator explicitly assigns the Moderator role.
- Assigned Moderator: moderation operations require both the current assignment
  and current Premium entitlement. Check both on the server. Losing either
  stops those operations; operator administration remains a separate role.

INVITATIONS
Use authenticated acceptance of a private, expiring invite. Do not reveal plan
contents in an invite URL before acceptance. Recheck the inviter's authority
when it is accepted. Store invitation secrets safely and make revocation work.
Do not send invitations merely because someone typed a name in the local roster.


4. COMPLETE OFFLINE PLAN DOWNLOADS
-----------------------------------
The goal is an explicit Download plan action, not reliance on data that happened
to appear in an online screen.

A plan package should identify:
- Plan ID, published revision, download time, and device/account ownership.
- Every plan instruction, named location, coordinate, and permitted contact.
- Supply quantities, assignments, and shared status included in that revision.
- All attachments promised as part of the selected package.
- A manifest of required files, sizes, and integrity checks.

Download into temporary storage, verify completeness, then replace the current
offline package atomically. Keep the last complete package if an update stops.
Do not label a partly downloaded plan as ready.

Reading a downloaded plan should not require a live login, subscription check,
or cloud response each time the app opens. Native local access can use the
phone's appropriate local protection. An expired network session should not
make the emergency copy inaccessible.

Show the saved revision and last successful download time. Offline, the app
cannot know whether another person has published a newer version.

DISTINGUISH THREE DOWNLOAD STATES
1. Plan text, locations, supplies, contacts, and supported files.
2. Offline map imagery/region data.
3. Offline road/trail routing data and its routing engine.

The first does not provide the second or third. A truthful intermediate label
is "Plan downloaded; maps not downloaded." A complete offline-map promise
requires licensed map packages and separate device tests.

SUBSCRIPTION EXPIRY
Stopping payment must not erase or hide downloaded plans. Renewal cancellation
and the end of the paid term are different states. Preserve existing drafts;
do not discard a user's work because a purchase check failed.

REMOVING A MEMBER
Removing access stops future server reads, downloads, updates, and writes.
An offline phone cannot receive that revocation until it reconnects, and an
exported file or screenshot cannot reliably be recalled. Sharing UI must say
that members can keep previously downloaded copies. Avoid promising remotely
revocable offline copies.

SHARED EDITING WHILE OFFLINE
The first shared-service milestone can require internet to publish edits.
Later, store offline changes as explicit drafts/outbox operations. They must
be labeled "Not yet shared" and remain distinct from the last published plan.
The server rechecks membership, edit permission, entitlement, and version
when the device reconnects. Any offline drafting grace is a policy decision,
not a substitute for server authorization.

CONFLICTS
Use stable IDs, server revisions, and unique operation IDs. Two people changing
the same meeting place must receive a conflict, not a silent overwrite. Treat
latitude and longitude together as one value. Retrying an operation must not
duplicate a supply or message. Old drafts must not resurrect deleted content.
Devices returning after a long absence should fetch a fresh complete snapshot
before attempting to reconcile their preserved drafts.


4A. PERSONAL PDF AND VIDEO GUIDES
---------------------------------
CURRENT LOCAL LIBRARY
- Import PDF documents and MP4, MOV, or M4V video files through the device's
  file picker. Copy actual file bytes into Black Box's local storage; keeping
  only a web link, file-picker reference, or cloud placeholder is insufficient.
- The native app uses a dedicated folder in its Documents directory, with a
  prototype limit of 1 GiB per file. The browser uses IndexedDB, with a limit
  of 100 MiB per file. Available device/browser space can impose smaller limits.
- Keep imported originals. Removing a guide from Black Box removes the app's
  copy and metadata, not the original source file.
- A source stored in iCloud Drive or another online provider may first need
  to download while connected. Only a completed local copy is available later.
- Import failures and missing/unreadable files must be visible and must not
  be presented as ready for use. A failed import must not damage existing guides.
- Open saved copies through the operating system's viewer or the browser.
  PDF viewing and video playback depend on the available viewer, the file's
  actual encoding, and the device. An accepted extension alone does not prove
  that a particular video will play. Test each guide on the intended device.
- This feature does not upload to a Black Box cloud service or share guides
  with other groups. Operating-system backup may include the native Documents
  folder according to the device's backup settings.
- No PDF manuals, videos, licensed guide collection, or emergency instructions
  are bundled with this update. Users supply their own files.

OFFLINE VERIFICATION AND BACKUPS
Browser storage is evictable and can be cleared. The Windows development
preview still needs its local launcher/server after restart; storing files in
IndexedDB does not make the whole development app independently launchable
offline. Native app installation, offline restart, PDF opening, video playback,
and low-storage behavior require real-device tests; they are not verified here.

Existing group JSON backups exclude guide media and the personal packing list.
Keep the original guide files separately. Use Copy list text for a readable
copy of personal packing items. Clearing app/browser storage or deleting the
app can remove these local items. Native device backup behavior is separate
from Black Box's existing JSON export.

FUTURE SHARED GUIDES
An authorized Premium Editor should be able to upload guide files into a
shared plan. An invited free reader must be able to download the whole plan,
including its supported guide files, and keep using completed local copies
without a monthly subscription. Group visibility, per-file authorization,
storage/bandwidth quotas, file manifests and integrity verification, interrupted
download recovery, and upload/distribution rights still need implementation.
The current personal guide library does not implement that shared-cloud flow.

4B. PERSONAL PACKING LIST
--------------------------
The current list is private to the local app/browser storage; it is not tied
to an authenticated account because accounts do not exist in this prototype.
It supports items, quantities, notes, packed states, counts, and simple filters.
Its separate save key leaves the existing group plan and backup format intact.
Saving failures must show the working list as unsaved and allow retry/copy.
The current app session retains failed drafts across tab changes, but closing
the app before a successful save can lose that draft. Corrupt stored records
must remain available for copying and must not be replaced with an empty list.

Version 0.6.0 adds separate Owned and Packed fields. Marking Packed implies
Owned; clearing Owned also clears Packed. Owning an item does not by itself
mark it packed. The existing personal save key is retained, reads accept
schema 1 and 2, and writes use schema 2. Schema-1 packed items migrate as owned;
unchecked older items remain unconfirmed as owned. This migration does not
alter the shared plan, its schema, or its JSON backup. Personal items and
guide files remain outside that group backup.

5. COMMUNITY AND INBOX
-----------------------
COMMUNITY: an online forum with categories, topics, replies, search, and
moderation. Suggested first categories are planning, equipment, outdoor skills,
and local preparation. A private group discussion and a public community post
are different audiences; the app must make the audience visible before posting.
Private plans, supply caches, contact details, and coordinates are not published
to the forum automatically.

Free posting and replying are confirmed. Free moderation is not allowed.
Moderator operations use an explicit administrator-granted role plus active
Premium, enforced on the server for every moderation action. A paid account
cannot appoint itself or gain access to another group's private information.
Operator administration is separate. None of these server roles is implemented
in the local prototype.

INBOX: durable messages with sender, recipient, subject, body, time, and reply
thread. Useful folders are Inbox, Sent, Drafts, and Archive. Live typing indicators
or presence are unnecessary. Sending/receiving new messages needs internet.
An optional offline draft/queue must say Draft or Queued until the server
accepts it; a notification is not proof that the recipient read a message.

Downloaded messages may be readable offline. A plan download should not silently
download an unlimited forum archive. Attachments, messaging access rules, and
retention need explicit limits.
Free inbox sending remains an open business decision, not a confirmed rule.

Apple's user-content guidance calls for filtering, reports with timely response,
blocking abusive users, and reachable contact information. Build these into
the forum/inbox release. Reporting, blocking, and account privacy controls
remain available without Premium. See reference A1.

7. TECHNICAL FOUNDATION AND BILLING
------------------------------------
Recommended direction, not a deployed service:
- Keep Flutter for the app.
- Move primary multi-plan data into a transactional native local database.
  Keep display preferences separate. Retain the old saved record and backups
  until migration is verified.
- Use an account service and a relational cloud database. Supabase Auth,
  Postgres, Row Level Security, and server mutation functions are a proposed
  fit for the account/group/plan relationships. See S1.
- Use explicit versioned downloads and upload operations for offline behavior.
  Selecting a cloud SDK does not automatically make a complete offline package.
- Keep privileged service credentials on the server; never embed them in the
  app or repository. Enforce permissions for database rows and stored files.
- Use verified purchase/renewal state for entitlement; never trust an editable
  client flag such as isSubscribed. Start with sandbox purchases before charging.
- Protect local and server data and implement recovery; the current prototype
  must not be described as an encrypted or crash-certified information vault.

The recommended first iPhone purchase route is an auto-renewing in-app
subscription, with clear ongoing value, price, period, and restoration.
Handle cancellation, expiry, billing grace, refunds, and duplicate/missed
server events. Alternative storefront payment arrangements are outside this
initial implementation. See A1 and A3.

Apps with accounts need an in-app account-deletion path. Define how authored
posts, private plans, and personal information in shared plans are handled;
ownership transfer alone does not settle personal-data deletion. Deleting an
account and canceling Apple billing are separate actions. See A2.

There is no connected cloud project, account system, or billing setup in this
archive. No messages, invites, purchases, or deployments were sent/performed.


8. COST AND PROFIT MODEL
--------------------------
One paid organizer may support many free readers. Budget for all users' storage
and downloads, not only the subscriber's own traffic.

Monthly operating contribution =
  subscription receipts after store fees/refunds/taxes
  minus cloud, storage, bandwidth, maps, messaging/email, moderation,
  support, and other operating costs.

This is a model to measure, not a forecast or guaranteed profit.
Important decisions before choosing prices:
- Typical plans and group size per paying user.
- Total and per-file attachment limits.
- Map download rights, file sizes, and provider charges.
- Revision retention and inactive-account/plan retention.
- Free-reader traffic and repeated downloads.
- Public-community moderation and support workload.

Start communication with text, measure actual use, and set published allowances.
The paid value should be ongoing planning, collaboration, and cloud service;
reading an existing emergency copy should not be interrupted by a paywall.

TestFlight builds become unavailable after 90 days. Treat them as test builds,
not a permanent app installation for a phone stored in a go bag. Production
distribution and periodic device/offline checks are part of the launch work.
See A4 and the researched launch priorities in LAUNCH_PLAN.txt.

The public community can follow private group sharing. Its moderation and
discovery requirements should not delay proving that a free family member can
receive and retain a complete shared plan.


10. RELEASE ACCEPTANCE CHECKS
-------------------------------
- The original plan survives library initialization without changing its
  storage keys. Unreadable records are retained, not replaced with defaults.
- Each plan's current, previous, and recovery records remain separate through
  switching, reload, failed writes, restore attempts, and archived state.
- Blank creation has You and no example supplies. Duplication clears only
  the copied shared Packed checks; import preserves the imported checks.
- Archive is reversible and cannot archive the current plan until switching.
- Family information saves only to the selected plan, survives reopen, and
  appears in that plan's JSON/text copies. Cancel discards the draft only.
- Legacy schemas 1/2 load with empty Family information; first schema-3 save
  preserves the old raw record as a previous copy. Do not silently downgrade.
- Personal pack and guide files stay common across plans and remain outside
  individual group backups. Switching does not change their content.
- The optional introduction can be started or dismissed without blocking the
  existing app; a failed help preference read/write does not alter plan data.
- Quick-tour Back, Next, Skip, Done, replay, and final Open Plan work in both
  appearances, with long text and larger text settings. Completion is not
  displayed as proof that the user's actual plan is ready.
- Help search finds relevant numbered instructions, topic expansion works,
  and direct Open buttons reach the intended screen without editing data.
- The guide-book, sun/moon, and question-mark shortcuts remain reachable;
  Appearance and About remain available through Help.
- Bundled Help can be read after opening the installed app without a network
  connection. Map imagery, saved-plan access, and guide playback keep their
  own separate offline requirements and checks.
- Existing local plans and backups survive migration with all fields intact.
- A new free invitee reads/downloads every authorized part without purchasing.
- A Premium Viewer cannot edit via the UI or by directly calling the API.
- A subscriber cannot see a different group's private plan by changing an ID.
- Joining one group does not reveal unshared plans from another group.
- A downloaded plan opens after a cold restart with Wi-Fi/cellular off.
- Expired login tokens and subscription expiry do not hide downloaded content.
- Interrupted or corrupt updates leave the previous complete package usable.
- Permission removal stops future server access; UI explains retained copies.
- Conflicting edits preserve both drafts and require deliberate resolution.
- Repeated upload requests do not duplicate data or send a message twice.
- An account switch does not expose the previous account's offline plans.
- An imported backup cannot forge membership or a paid entitlement.
- Report, block, and account deletion work for free and expired accounts.
- Free accounts can post/reply but cannot invoke moderator operations.
- Premium alone cannot grant moderation; both an explicit assignment and
  current entitlement are required, with separate operator administration.
- A free user's personal checklist can be edited without altering the shared
  plan's items or packed statuses; group backups remain independent.
- Personal checklist writes remain ordered across tab changes. Failed saves
  preserve the in-session draft, and corrupt stored data is not overwritten.
- Old personal schema-1 packed items migrate as owned; unchecked items remain
  unconfirmed. Schema-2 reads/writes preserve ownership, packing, and catalog IDs.
- Packed sets Owned, clearing Owned clears Packed, and I have this leaves a

- Adding a catalog item twice preserves one edited entry, including its
  quantity, notes, and existing ownership/packing status.
- Add & shop saves before offering Open Amazon/Open Amazon search; a save

- The unconfigured web preview uses ordinary links; unapproved phone builds

  origin, valid supplied full links, and the visible disclosure. Preview/IP

- A completed PDF/video import contains a local byte copy and survives a

- Missing, corrupt, unsupported, or incomplete files do not show a false
  ready status; interrupted imports leave existing guides intact.
- Each test phone opens a saved PDF and plays a saved supported video after
  a cold restart with Wi-Fi/cellular disabled.
- Removing a local guide leaves its original source file untouched.
- Browser eviction/storage limits and the development launcher's offline

- Shared-plan downloads include permitted guide files for free readers;

11. COMPETITOR RESEARCH
-------------------------
The earlier comparison below used primary product websites and developer-
authored listings reviewed on November 20, 2026 local time. The expanded
November 22 review and resulting launch priorities are in LAUNCH_PLAN.txt.
These are published claims, not independent tests or proof of market demand.

PrepperApp: shared household emergency planning, invitations, offline data,
mapped places, and an in-app/web community forum. Its forum allows posting
with a free account. This is a close overall feature comparison. [C1, C2]

Firefly: shared family preparedness information and offline tools. Its listing
keeps saved information readable after a trial and charges a one-time unlock
to continue adding/editing. The free-reader/paid-editor concept is therefore
already present in this market, with a different architecture/payment model. [C3]

Warrior Gardeners: multiple preparedness locations, inventories, scenarios,
team planning, and sharing choices for individual plans/resources. [C4]

ReadyPlan: scenario-based preparedness plans, family/team sharing, checklists,
and saved offline plan access. [C5]

SHTF: Life Prep: private groups, a community library, plans available offline,
and real-time chat/DM communication. [C6]

Our Plan Is: shared household plans and offline access, with online weather and
hazard information for saved places. [C7]

Conclusion: similar apps exist, including close combinations. Black Box can
compete through clear multi-group/multi-plan permissions, complete offline
copies for free readers, a useful paid-editor service, and straightforward
forum/inbox coordination. These are positioning goals, not proof of novelty
or market demand. An unmentioned feature on a marketing page is not proof that
a competitor lacks it.
